## 1.22.3 — Carried at rest, smoother on phones
- The Barrow Knight now carries its swords lowered like a knight at rest, angled forward and down with the edge to the ground and the flat of the blade showing. Each grip runs crosswise through a closed plated gauntlet, with the fingers wrapped around it, the thumb locked over them, and the back of the hand turned outward. The full-length greatsword is back.
- Phones: looking around by dragging is now smoother. On a slow frame, touch input arrives in bursts. The camera now eases through each burst over a few frames instead of jumping, so it no longer feels like it hangs and then lurches.
- Phones and tablets: when a heavy view slows the frame rate, the render resolution steps down slightly, and it climbs back once things run smoothly again.

## 1.22.2 — A proper grip
- The Barrow Knight holds its swords properly now. Each fist closes around the middle of the grip, with plated fingers stacked down the handle and the thumb locked over the top.
- Both blades hang vertically in line with the forearm instead of jutting forward, edge forward with the guard running front to back, so they stay vertical through the swing. The greatsword is now a long blade so it clears the ground when held this way.

## 1.22.1 — The knight's limbs
- The Barrow Knight's arms and legs are rebuilt as heavy articulated plate to match its chest and shoulders. The arms have three overlapping upper-arm lames, a domed elbow cop with a round fan wing, a vambrace that swells over the forearm with a bronze ridge, and a belled gauntlet cuff over plated hands with jointed fingers.
- The legs have a swelling, lamed thigh plate with a bronze ridge, a large pointed knee cop with a heart-shaped side wing and lames above and below, a greave that swells at the calf with a medial ridge and bronze rims, ankle rings, and boots of five overlapping trimmed lames ending in a pointed toe cap.

## 1.22.0 — Hollow steel, bone and rot
- The Barrow Knight is now a real suit of armour pieced together from separate plates: a horned great-helm with a burning T-shaped visor and breathing holes, gorget rings, a breastplate with a raised plackart and medial ridge, a backplate, three fauld lames, layered pauldrons, vambraces, elbow and knee cops, gauntlets, tassets, cuisses, greaves and segmented sabatons. Every plate edge carries engraved, patinated bronze trim and rivets.
- Nothing is inside the armour. The gaps between plates show black emptiness lit by a faint corrupted ember glow, and dark wisps leak from the neck. The steel reflects its surroundings, with grime and rust worked in, under a long tattered black cape.
- The knight's swords are reforged with diamond-section steel blades, bronze guards and pommels, and leather-wrapped grips.
- The draugr's bones are aged: crevices are shaded, the surface is pitted and hairline-cracked, grime streaks run down, and moss grows in the hollows. Lank hair hangs from under its iron cap.
- The ghoul is redesigned: a starved, hunched body with ribs, sternum, collarbones, shoulder blades and spine showing under the skin, a hollow belly, a long gaunt skull with a hanging jaw and teeth, knotted elbows and knees, and long hooked hands and feet. Its dead grey-blue skin has green rot, purple bruising at the joints, faint veins and sores, and a sheen only in the folds. It now wears three strands of bone and bead necklaces, bead bracelets on both wrists, a filthy wrap and torn rags, and its mouth glows violet.

## 1.21.0 — The dead of the tombs
- Draugr are rebuilt as rotting skeletal warriors: a grinning skull with dark hollow sockets and cold sparks for eyes under a riveted iron cap, an open leather vest over bare ribs, studded pauldrons, sinew on the bones, rag strips at the belt, a round plank shield and a notched sword or bearded axe.
- New: the Barrow Knight. A towering armoured dead lord in a great-helm with a red light in its visor slit, layered pauldrons and plate, a tattered black skirt and a rotted mantle. It carries a notched greatsword and a longsword. Usually one guards each great hall, and two guard every throne room. Tough and slow, with heavy swings and rich grave goods.
- New: the Ghoul. A fast, hunched corpse-eater with grey-blue starved skin, glowing violet eyes, a gaping mouth, lank black hair, a bone necklace and long purple-stained claws. Ghouls prowl the tombs among the draugr.

## 1.20.0 — Centuries in the dark
- Dust has settled over every floor, ledge and stone that faces up. White mineral streaks and lichen creep down the walls, and the stone is pitted and eroded.
- Parts of chamber vaults have caved in, leaving a ragged hole with daylight falling through onto a mound of fallen blocks and earth.
- Earth and roots have burst through walls in places. Blocks have been pushed out of the walls, with others fallen at their feet.
- Some hall pillars have snapped, their drums lying across the floor, and the arches they held are gone.
- Coffin lids have been shoved aside, with bones spilled beside them. Iron chains, cages and sconces are rusted.
- Small mineral stalactites hang from the old ceilings and vault ribs.

## 1.19.0 — Tomb interiors: light, stone and atmosphere
- Real torchlight: every torch, brazier, candle and lantern lights the stone around it, and walls and pillars cast shadows, so light pools on the floors and corridors fall into darkness between torches. Wet stone catches highlights from the flames, and soot blackens the stone above each torch.
- Arches over corridors, doorways and hall arcades are now built from individual wedge stones with a keystone. The bricks of the vaulted corridors follow the curve of the vault.
- Finer stonework: thinner, crumbling mortar; grainy stone faces; cracked and chipped blocks; broken and sunken flagstones; dirt gathered along the walls.
- Roots hang from cracks in the vaults. Smoke curls up from every torch. Water drips in the drowned cave and in Grim-Eye.
- Heaps of bones with hollow-eyed skulls, iron gibbet cages hanging from the vaults, debris along the walls, and stone rib beams across chamber ceilings.
- Shafts of daylight are now soft, dusty beams.
- Cave columns are true stalactite-and-stalagmite pillars, and the glowing pool lights its cavern.
- Every corridor and chamber has torches at regular intervals.

## 1.18.3 — Rooted standing stones
- The four standing stones before each tomb now stand rooted in the ground instead of hovering on a flat-cut base.

## 1.18.2 — Grounded tombs
- Tomb entrances now settle into the land: on slopes the boulders reach down and bed into the ground, and the path stones, steps, torch posts and rune stones follow the terrain instead of hovering.
- A tomb's halls, light beam and chests are always hidden once you're outside, however you leave (walking out, returning home or respawning), so nothing hangs in the eastern sky.

## 1.18.1 — The tombs, made real
- Stonework has true surface depth: recessed mortar, worn and chipped block edges, and uneven, tilted flagstones with sunken grout.
- Damp streaks run down the tomb walls with a wet sheen. Corners and the feet of walls fall into soft shadow.
- Torches, braziers and candles now burn with living, flickering flames and a soft glow, instead of solid cones.
- Fine dust drifts in the air of the tombs.
- Entrance boulders are now split, angular rock with flat cleaved faces, sharp edges and moss on top (frost in the mountains). Cave rock, stalactites and columns are smooth natural stone.

## 1.18.0 — The tombs, rebuilt
- New tomb entrances: a carved stone portal set into a heap of mossy boulders, with an iron-banded door standing ajar, glowing runes in the lintel, torch posts, standing stones along a flagstone path and mist at the threshold.
- Every tomb has its own look inside:
  - Barrow of Ketil: a warm, torchlit crypt with banners and braziers.
  - Tomb of Grim-Eye: violet torches, hanging chains and reflective puddles.
  - Tombs high in the mountains: frost halls with icicles and cold blue light.
  - New tomb, Deepfall Caves: a drowned cavern with rough rock walls, stalactites, mossy floors and a glowing turquoise pool ringed with crystals.
- Real stonework: block-and-mortar walls and irregular flagstone floors, arched brick vaults over the corridors, arched doorways (some with portcullises), columns with bases and capitals, and tall pillared halls with arcades.
- Lantern niches, iron sconces, candle clusters, cobwebs, rubble, skulls, urns, sarcophagi and shafts of light falling through cracks in the vaults.
- Each treasure hall now ends in a raised dais with a stone throne beneath a carved, gilded sun.
- The tomb map is now an inked parchment floor plan with hatched rock and gridded floors, revealed as you explore. The big map (M) shows the explored part.

**Fjordborn 1.12.0: launcher, ships, ore veins and a castle-galleon**

- **New launcher:** the desktop game now opens to a launcher with news, your version and a Play button. Updates download there with a progress bar, then the Update button installs them and restarts.
- **Shipwright Dock:** build it on the shore facing the sea, then build ships there: the small Faering, the Karve and the great Drakkar. Ships launch off the end of the dock. Each one has a hold: open your bag while aboard to store things. Everything for the dock and the Faering comes from the Sunmeads.
- **Smelting:** a Charcoal Kiln turns wood into charcoal, and a Smelter turns ore and charcoal into ingots. The Forge now shapes ingots into gear.
- **Ore in every land:** tin in the Strand, copper in the Sunmeads, ember in the Gloamwood and rime ore in the Hoarfells. Deposits are much bigger, half sunk into the earth, with real veins of metal, and give plenty of ore and stone. Copper and tin make bronze, for a Bronze Pick and a Bronze Axe.
- **The Drowned Colossus rebuilt** as a castle-galleon with stone towers, turrets and spires, tall masts with torn sails and rigging, ivy, and candlelit windows. The holds have rotting timber, hull ribs, chandeliers, flooded cabins, light through broken decks and a drowned salon full of gold. Chests on the terraces and in the holds hold gold, Sea Garnets and rare materials.
- **Unlock as you find:** recipes and buildings appear as you first pick up what they need, with a notice telling you what is new.
- **Quick bar:** eight slots for tools, weapons, food and anything usable. It fills as you craft, and the bag has + Bar buttons to change it. Food on the bar shows its count and is eaten with a tap.
- **Base lighting:** Lantern Post and Candle Stand, and wall torches now light the room. The desktop game shows up to six lights at once.
- **Sound:** relaxing music with silences between pieces, plus wind, surf and birds. Villagers murmur when they talk. There are volume sliders for master, music, effects, ambience and voices.
- **Camera settings:** look sensitivity, field of view and invert up/down. You can now look up at the sky.
- **Realistic water and sky:** clear shallows and dark depths, layered ripples, sky reflections, sun glitter and thin foam. The sky has proper haze and sun glow, and at night stars, the Milky Way and a lit moon.
- **Movement:** jump onto boulders and stand on them. Swimming is now a breaststroke, and you tread water when still.
- **Ground:** fallen branches look like real branches, and there are 35% fewer loose sticks and stones.
- **Phone:** the sun is dimmer. The home-screen app checks for updates on the title screen.
- **Credits and licences** page. If something goes wrong, the game saves at once.
